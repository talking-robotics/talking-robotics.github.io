abstract: >
    The past decade has seen remarkable advances in surgical robotics, which improve patient outcomes and provide intuitive and dexterous control for surgeons. Many surgical robots utilize cable-driven mechanisms to enable light and compact arms and instruments. However, cables also introduce considerable inaccuracy in control due to the stretch of cables and motor backlash. Moreover, because of the miniaturization and sterilization of the surgical instrument, integration of sensors within the end-effector is difficult and costly. Thus, surgeons have to rely mainly on endoscopic vision when operating surgical robots, and automation is also hindered. This talk will focus on my approaches to improving the closed-loop control and perception for surgical robots. My talk will begin with an introduction to an efficient data-driven position calibration for a cable-driven surgical robot. We reduced the input dimensions of the data-driven models and improved the explainability by performing an ablation study on input features to identify crucial and irrelevant features. Next, I will describe my development of distal force sensing for surgical robots trained by a motor-cable force actuation system. This system seeks to learn a force-estimation model without the need for proximal force sensors on surgical instruments. I will conclude my talk by sharing my vision for advancing feasible data-driven control and perception of surgical robots, to provide better control for surgeons and for future automation in robotic surgeries.
